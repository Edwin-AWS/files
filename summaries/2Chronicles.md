# Second Chronicles

## Introduction

### Title

- The title **“Chronicles”** is an English rendering of the Latin word from _"Chronicon"_ from Jerome's Vulgate.
- In original Hebrew, the title "_Dibre hayyamim_" literally, _"events of the days"_ was used and was abbreviated from "_Sepher dibre hayyamim_", literally **"book of events of the days"**
- Originally a single book with 1 Chronicles in the Hebrew  Septuagint (in the same fashion as 1 & 2 Samuel and 1 & 2 Kings,) the book was divided into two in the 4th century by the Greek translators.
- The given title _"Paraleipomenon"_, _"of things omitted"_ referred to things omitted in Samuel and Kings.

### Authorship

- Similarities in writing style , language and a general point of view suggests unity of authorship with the books of Ezra and Nehemiah.
- The last two verses of 2 Chronicles 36, ending in mid sentence are actually identical to the first 3 verses of Ezra 1, pointing to a continuation of one book just divided.
- The most probable author is therefore Ezra.

### Historical Setting

- With a period of completion set in the late 5th century BC, the focus of 2nd Chronicles is on the kings of Judah, starting with Solomon, just before the split of the kingdom under his son Rehoboam.
- Particular focus is placed on the lineage of David in the nation of Judah, the Southern kingdom, with only a passing glance at the kings of Israel,  the Northern kingdom, established under king Jeroboam to the extent that their interests merged or differed.

### Theme

- The central theme of the book of 2nd Chronicles is **_faithfulness to God, temple worship and the consequences of obedience versus disobedience._** God also values an offender who repents wholeheartedly.
- Chapters 1-9 focus on the reign of Solomon,  his asking for wisdom, the building of the temple, its dedication ending with his achievements,  the visit of the queen of Sheba and Solomon's death.
- Chapters 10 - 12 narrates the split of the kingdom under Rehoboam, with the North being controlled by Jeroboam
- The rest of the chapters (13 - 35) focus on the alternate kings of Judah, good and bad, their idolatry and reforms
- In Chapter 36, the last kings reject God, Babylon conquers Jerusalem and destroys the temple. The people are taken into exile but the book ends with hope:Cyrus the Persian king allows Jews to return and rebuild  the temple.

### The Message

- God though the Chronicler sets forth His purpose in the experience of the chosen people showing the decline of the kingdom and even the destruction of the holy temple and its sacred rituals as a result of sin.
- Dependence on God leads to blessings and its never too late to turn back to God. All choices have consequences and though God disciplines, he doesn't abandon.
- _"The obedience that Christ rendered is exactly the obedience that God requires from human beings today. It was the obedience of a son. He served His Father in willingness and freedom, and with love, because it was the right thing for Him to do."_ *(Ellen G White: Present Truth January 12,  1899, par. 10).*

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father in heaven, you have a sacred work for each and everyone of us. A work that uplifts the banner of Christ, that strengthens the weak knees,  that sows the good seed. Gracious are you among your people, sinful as we daily are. When we go astray, teach us to repent and restore us in the name of Jesus, Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 1 — Solomon Asks God for Wisdom

### Summary

God strengthes and magnifies Solomon. Solomon offers many burnt offerings at Gibeon. In a dream, he requests wisdom of God. He accumulates large numbers of chariots, horsemen,  gold and cedar.

### Key Lessons

**1. A gradual restoration (vs 1-6)**

- God strengthens and magnifies Solomon exceedingly as he begins to reign.
- Solomon sought to restore tabernacle centred worship, setting off and offering "a thousand burnt offerings" on the brasen altar which Moses through Bezaleel made and was still at Hebron.
- Of note is the fact that the Ark of the Covenant, having been carried from Kiriathjearim, was now at Jerusalem in a tent made by David.
- Under Saul the people had not inquired of it. _"The repairer of the breach, the restorer of paths to dwell in."_ *(Isaiah 58:12)*
- These two critical worship instruments were now at different places and Solomon was taking steps, one at a time to reconnect Israel with true worship.
- Modern worship practices (music, liturgy, dress, diet) may mark departure from proper and acceptable practices before God.
- There is need for any leader to look for the old paths, walk in them and restore the right spirit in Christ’s followers.
- Thank God worship is now no longer centred on places, but "in spirit and truth".

**2. Far better than riches (vs 7-12)**

- After the offerings at Hebron,  God appeared to Solomon in a dream saying "ask what I shalt give you"
- Solomon asks for wisdom in leading God's people,  which is granted with a bonus of "riches, honor and wealth" above any man to ever walk the earth.
- Leaders should be selected based on wisdom wealth rather than material wealth.
- "_The deepest poverty, the greatest self-denial, with His approval, is better than riches, honors, ease and friendship without it_" *(Ellen G. White: Great Controversy 622.2)*
- We have a promise in Joshua 1:8 and 2 Timothy 3:14 that consistent and  habitual scripture reading results in an endowment of wisdom, for all wisdom is of God.
- True wisdom should bring honor to God.
- _The wisdom of this world, of which worldly men are proud, in which they glory, brings no honor to God. Let not any man deceive himself by thinking that he has superior wisdom_ *(Ellen G. White: 14 Letters and Manuscripts 165, 1899, par. 50)*
- Although Solomon's reign was characterised by peace, he fortified Israel's cities (Hazor, Megiddo, Millo, etc) and borders against enemies (vs 14).
- God had in Deuteronomy 17: 16-17 specifically forbidden multiplication of horses, wives and gold, which Solomon went on to do. (vs 14-15)
- That God allows something to happen does not necessarily mean He approves of it. Every work shall be tried by fire.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father God, we have allowed the status quo to overrule our spirituality. Lukewarmness and indifference defines our daily spirituality. Open our eyes to things we have overlooked. Grant us the wisdom that comes from daily beholding your face. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 2 — Solomon Prepares to Build the Temple

### Summary

Solomon prepares both labour and requisite material for commencement of building the temple. He outsources some from Huram king of Tyre, getting enthusiastic positive feedback.

### Key Lessons

**1. As lively stones (vs 1-2,  17-18)**

- Solomon determined to build the temple assembles a 153 600 strong labour force.
- Any successful story starts with a determination, a purposing.
- As Ezra **_prepared his heart_** to seek the law, do it and teach it (Ezra 6:5), as Daniel _**purposed**_ not to defile himself indulging in the king's delicacies (Daniel 1:8), we also _**as lively stones,**_  _building up a spiritual house_, needs the same resolve.
- As much as Solomon sought to build the temple,  he also sought to build "_an house for his kingdom_"
- A balance ought to be struck between serving God and providing for the family.
- _To a large degree the happiness of men and women and the success of the church depend upon home influence...._ *(Ellen G. White: Adventist Home 353.3)*
- Strangers in Israel's fold were called to give their hand to the Temple building work.(vs 2-3, 17-18)
- As they lent a hand and participate in things spiritual, non believers are drawn to the faith. As a bonus, it kindles and fosters harmonious living and peaceful societies.

**2. The name of the Lord, My God**

- In his appeal to Huram king of Tyre, Solomon uses phrases like, "_I build an house to the Lord my God..burn before him sweet incense,... continual shewbread,...for burnt offerings morning and evening_
- He goes on to suggest that  God does not necessarily need a physical house, "_seeing the Heaven, and the heaven of heavens cannot contain him"_ (vs 6), neither dwelleth _in temples made with hands;.._ *(Acts 17:24-26)*
- God, in Isaiah 1:11 says _"..I delight not in the blood of bullocks, or of lambs, or of he goats.._" and declares _"if i were hungry, i would not tell thee: for the world is mine, and the fulness thereof._" *(see Psalm 50:12).*
- Solomon understood this thus  to him, the Temple was not built for God, but **"_..to the Name of the Lord my God..._**
- It is our acts which bring glory to God's name. When our tongues confess that _Jesus Christ_ (a name above all names) _is Lord"_, the name of God is glorified. *(Philippians 2:11)*
- Even in designing the priestly garments for Aaron the High priest, God says they should be "**_for glory and for beauty)_**" *(Exodus 28:10, 40)*
- Our lives should magnify, uplift and glorify the Almighty's Name.
- Physical temples though we may build, (and we should) the temple, which our bodies are, should be prioritized **"_for the spirit of God dwelleth in you_"**

**3. Excellency: A great house for a great God**

- To Huram, Solomon continues,  **"For I build a great house, for great is our God above all Gods."**
- He requests for _a man cunning to work in gold..._ (in the same fashion as Bezaleel,  and Aholiab, (Jews) were called in Exodus 31:1-11  among other things.
- A hidden sermon is in Solomon's words, "**...great God above all gods**",  a clear declaration to an apparent nonbeliever that Solomon  considers his God superior.
- What is heartening is that in Huram's response (vs 12)  he acknowledges God as He _"that made heaven and earth"_, a language that any believer, prophet, apostle or angel uses in recognizing God's primary attribute. *(See Exodus 20:11, Jonah 1:9, Acts 14:15, Revelation 14:7)*
- Anything dedicated to God should be done in such Magnificence and Excellency as He deserves.
- Today, do not for fear of being called judgemental, be ashamed of the gospel, of calling sin by its right name, of the remnant of our faith, of the Sabbath,  of the health message, of His second coming and of the Spirit of Prophecy.
- Huram believed that God gave Israel Solomon as king out of love for them (vs 11)
- How comforting a thought it would be for any leader today to be considered a blessing to God's people and all who love the Lord.
- Solomon promises to pay for the labour in vs 10
- Labourers need to be adequately compensated for their effort.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Almighty God, may your Holy name be magnified in our daily lives. When called upon to help in your work most precious,  may our only response be: Here i am Lord, sent me. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 3 — Construction of the Temple Begins

### Summary

Temple construction begins on Mount Moriah.

### Key Lessons

**1. The genesis of Temple construction**

- Solomon commences building the temple on Mount Moriah,  on Ornan's threshing floor, in the 4th year of his reign, (believed to be 967BC, about 480years after the exodus. *(1 Kings 6:1)*
- History is ordered, purposeful and divinely guided. Abraham's Mount Moriah's sacrifice, the staying of the plague at Ornan's threshing floor and God choosing it as temple site proves it.
- The apparent delay in temple building was largely due to the fact that _"All the masonry and hewing was done offsite.The Jewish temple was built of hewn stones quarried out of the mountains; and every stone was fitted for its place in the temple, hewed, polished, and tested before it was brought to Jerusalem. And when all were brought to the ground, the building went together without the sound of axe or hammer"._
- Believers "_are living stones, quarried out from the world by the truth; and the great Master Builder, the Lord of the temple, is now hewing and polishing them, and fitting them for their respective places in the spiritual temple"_ *(Ellen G. White: Counsels to the Church 287.5)*

**2. From Tabernacle to Temple (vs 3-16)**

- The dimensions of the new temple,  as given to David was just a doubling of the original tabernacle blueprint in *(Exodus 25:9)*
- The most holy place, (vs 13:18) was a perfect cube, denoting divine perfection. God calls upon believers to be perfect as He is perfect *(Mathew 5:42)*
- A veil of blue, purple and crimson/scarlet (in Exodus 26:31), which in the post exilic temple would be rent in twain at the death of Christ on the cross *(Mathew 27:51),* signify a new way to approach God.

**3. The Temple pillars (vs 17)**

- Two named pillars, Jachin (He shall establish) on the South and Boaz (In Him is strength) on the North of the Temple porch were constructed.
- These probably meant that God would establish the temple/kingdom in His strength.
- Both the earthly tabernacle and temple were a pattern of the Heavenly one.
- Abraham rejoiced to see Christ's day and was glad. Let us book a date in the everlasting New Jerusalem temple, wherein dwelleth righteousness.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Glorious Father in heaven, we thank you for showing us your pattern for hope's sake. You have prepared good things for us from the beginning.  Help us not to miss the mark, and tabernacle with you forevermore. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 4 — The Temple's Furnishings

### Summary

Temple Instruments,  vessels and utensils including the bronze altar,  the molten sea, bronze lavers, ten candle stands and tables are completed

### Key Lessons

**1. An elevated scale (vs 1-8)**

- As compared to the tabernacle by Moses, the grandeur of the Temple's instruments and utensils is evident.
- The bronze altar (vs 1, Exodus 27:1) was about 4 times bigger while there were now ten lavers, candles stands and tables (vs 2, 7, 8 Exodus 39:18, 25:31-40, 25:23-30) where there was one. A molten sea (vs 2) is also  introduced.
- In the brasen altar, we see our Savior's ultimate sacrifice on the cross, a lamb dying to cleanse us of our sin while the molten sea and laver depicts baptism of Christ and the believers, a renewal.
- In the candlesticks,  Christ shines as the light of the world while in the tables of shewbread,  He becomes the bread of life. *(John 6:35)*
- The similitude of 12 oxen  probably represents the 12 tribes, with 3 apiece facing each cardinal point

**2. Finishing the work (vs 11)**

- _"Huram finished the work that he was to make for king Solomon"_
- A full  7 years, the temple and its furnishings were being crafted. *(1 Kings 6:38)*
- Huram's dedication to duty needs to be emulated and replicated in believers today if God's church is to succeed in its mission, both missionary and church building.
- Blessed are they who begin a good thing and see to its fruition and like Paul to ultimately say "I have fought a good fight."

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father in heaven, we commit our leaders into your hands. Inspire them with such dedication as to see to the completion of your churchs projects in their diversity. May your house be filled with vessels worth of your Name. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 5 — The Ark Brought Into the Temple

### Summary

The dedicated things are brought into the temple.  Israel's leadership brings in the ark. Lots of sacrifices and praising song is done.

### Key Lessons

**1. God's house, a place for one accord**

- On completion of the temple,  Solomon brings in all such things as David had dedicated for temple use.
- He assembles all the leadership of Israel and brought the ark from Zion where it was in a tent.
- Everyone, king, leaders, priests and levites participate, proving the centrality of the house of God to all and sundry.
- The house of God unifies and all are called upon to participate in worship.
- Gratitude induces liberality. Countless lambs were sacrificed in celebration.

**2. Centrality of God's Law in Worship**

- After the completion of the magnificent temple, the main highlight was the bringing in of the ark of the Covenant, (vs 2-10). This was the primary reason the temple was built in the first place. *(2 Samuel 7:1-2)*
- Even though it originally contained the rod of Aaron that budded and the pot of manna, (Hebrews 9:4) it still maintained its sacredness while it now contained only the two tables of stone (vs 10)
- God's immutable law (the ten commandments) is therefore at the centre of all worship.
- _The ark was merely a receptacle for the tables of the law, and the presence of these divine precepts gave to it its value and sacredness_  *(Ellen G. White: Great Controversy 433.2)*
- _The law of God is as sacred as God Himself. It is a revelation of His will, a transcript of His character, the expression of divine love and wisdom. The harmony of creation depends upon the perfect conformity of all beings, of everything, animate and inanimate, to the law of the Creator._  *(Ellen G. White: Patriarchs and Prophets 52.2)*
- The Priests and Levites were Sanctified before carrying the ark and performing other duties. (vs 11, 13)
- When everything is done lawfully,  the presence of the Lord is guaranteed. The temple was filled with the presence of the Lord, His glory.
- Unity is a basis of attracting God's presence. When the trumpeters and singers,  _**As One**_,  as when the disciples were in one accord at Pentecost( Acts2:1), the presence of God descended.
- God's church today needs harmony and unity for God's presence to guide it.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Glorious Father, shine in our hearts. Fill our mouths with praise and our countenances with your Glory. May you be crowned king to rule our hearts. Fashion our characters unto Christlikeness. May we never forget your Law forevermore. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 6 — Solomon's Prayer of Dedication

### Summary

Solomon blesses the people and God. He goes on to offer a lengthy prayer while kneeling on a bronze scaffold, concluding by calling upon God to take occupation of the dedicated temple.

### Key Lessons

**1. Unseen,  but ever present**

- Solomon, after blessing the congregation delves into a brief history about God's past dealings with Israel.
- Although no man has seen God *(Exodus 33:20, John 1:18, 1 John 4:12),* His voice has been heard and His acts seen *(Exodus 19:19, Deuteronomy 4:12)*
- God can allow or prevent certain situations from materializing. He chose the temple site but prevented the builder (David, vs 5-9). If things do not go as you envisaged, leave room for God's will.
- Even when "disappointed", continue doing the necessary, like David did, organising for temple he couldn't build.
- Reverence is requisite.  Solomon, though a king himself, knelt before the King of kings

**2. God's promises always stand .**

- Solomon alludes to promises made to David about both a son king and the building of a temple which things God had fulfilled.
- What God promises, He is able to perform. Wait upon the Lord, "_though it tarry, wait for it, because it will surely come"_ *(Habakkuk 2:3)*
- When tempted to say "He tarries" remember,  :
- _"We have nothing to fear for the future, except as we shall forget the way the Lord has led us, and his teaching in our past history."_ *(Ellen G. White: Testimonies volume 9, 10.2)*

**3. Hear from Heaven**

- Solomon, on his knees repeatedly calls upon God to hear from Heaven, making 7 specific requests God needed to hear when people prayed towards the temple.
- Pagan temples contained their physical idols but in the temple, God's name dwells there. (vs 20) He is not contained there. He hears from heaven. (vs 21, 23, 25)
- His presence is on condition of obedience and repentance.
- People had to orient their prayers towards the temple, a symbol of God's presence.
- Daniel prayed 3 times daily towards the temple in Jerusalem (Daniel 6:10). A pagan sun worship tradition has gained ground though Ezekiel 8:16) where people continue to worship the sun towards the East.
- Disasters are characterised as  divine judgments for sin. (Legal disputes, (vs 22), military defeat (vs 24), famine (vs 26) plagues ( vs 28) and foreigners' supplications (vs 29)
- Sin is the primary cause of our troubles. Its not our relatives or enemies or bosses. The remedy is not sacrifices but a humble  repentant heart.
- We no longer need the physical temple to pray towards, but through Christ, to make our supplications known. _Looking unto Jesus, the author and Finisher of our faith_ *(Hebrews 12:2)*
- Leaders, like Solomon need to intercede for their people in prayer, as Christ also did in John 17.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father in heaven, you are He that hears our prayer. Whenever we go astray, may we remember to call upon your name, reverently, wherever we maybe. Hear us when we pray in Jesus' name. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 7 — God Accepts the Temple

### Summary

God accepts Solomon's supplications. Dedicatory sacrifices are offered. God appears once more to Solomon in a dream, promising him prosperity on condition of obedience.

### Key Lessons

**1. God can directly and instantly answer your prayer**

- Fire came down from heaven and glory filled the temple as a token of an answer to Solomon's prayer, consuming the sacrifice as happened with Elijah on Mt Carmel,  *(1 Kings 18:36)* and Leviticus 9:23 at Aaron's dedication of the tabernacle.
- One of the greatest moments for a believer is to have one's prayer answered instantly and exactly as requested.
- The dedication coincided with the feast of tabernacles,  thus the festival went on for 14 days (vs 8-9)
- Fullness of peace and joy is experienced where true love for and reverence of God exists. This, the world cannot provide. Israel was merry at the feast.
- So much was being offered such that an extra sacrificial spot had to be created to accommodate other offerings.

**2. Conditional promises**

- God appeared again to Solomon in a dream  addressing his specific prayer points, (vs 13).
- God promised to _"hear from Heaven and forgive and heal their land"_ on condition of humility,  prayer, repentance.
- People called by God's name should bear certain expected characteristics. Sadly in the world today, the line between believers and worldlings is fast getting invisible. Surely the love of many is getting cold
- It is essential when praying to be forthright with God and  have specific prayer points
- God gives a daily supply of blessings upon us, a daily bread, which he may bestow or withdraw. Constant communion with Him keeps it flowing.
- Even though God has given a general condition for all people called by His name (vs 14),  He goes on to give a personalized condition to Solomon saying "and as for thee..." (17).
- God likes to take people to the level of an individual.  Salvation is a personal matter. One's actions determine one's destiny.
- Each promise to us therefore bears a warning and a judgement.(vs 19)
- God would as readily disregard the respect he gives to places called by His name if the people fail to reverence his name. He hates idolatry. In 2 Kings 25:9, this magnificent temple would be destroyed by fire because of Israel's disobedience.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Loving Father in Heaven, thank you for the assurance of answering our prayers. Thank you for the wonderful promises you have presented to us. May thy glory continue to shine from our body temples forevermore. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 8 — Solomon's Building Projects

### Summary

Solomon builds cities rejected by Huram. He transforms the wilderness into storage cities. He receives tribute from the remnant of the Canaanites with Israelites occupying leadership positions. He builds a house for his Egyptian wife. Priests and Levites get organised into  courses. Huram helps him to bring in gold from Ophir.

### Key Lessons

**1. Disagreements between allies (vs 2, 18)**

- Solomon and Huram had strong relations *(1 Kings 5:12)* The alliance evidently never ceased.
- As payment for the work on the temple building project, Solomon had given Huram 20 cities which he rejected because _they pleased him not_ *(1 Kings 9:12)*
- Solomon built them and caused Israelites to live in them. The fact that mutual cooperation continued (8:18) even after the disagreement is commendable.
- Good relations should not cease because of a single disagreement. Differences should be ironed out and cordial relations continue.

**2. Everything should be wisely utilized (vs 4-6)**

- Solomon built storage and chariot cities in the wilderness.
- The wilderness could easily have been considered useless land but in his wisdom, he built strategic grain reserves and fenced cities
- Christ in John 6:12 after feeding the 5000 saying to the disciples: **_"Gather up the fragments that remain, that nothing be lost."_**
- Every available resource should be utilized for advancement of God's kingdom.
- Even those individuals often looked down upon in God's house have a critical role they can play in the salvation of souls if all things are carefully considered.

**3. The errors of the wise (vs  11)**

- Solomon desired many wives, *(2 Chronicles 11:23)* including Pharaoh's daughter. God had forbidden both polygamy and intermarriage *(Malachi 2:15, Matthew 19:4, Deuteronomy 17:17)*
- In trying not to defile a sacred place, he had to build her a house out of the city of David.
- A single dominant weakness should not lead to flagrant irreverent disregard of other sacred duties.
- Even the wise and the respected can make errors thus the standard for believers should only be Christ.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father in heaven, after adhering to you for so long, give us the strength to finish the course, making the most of every opportunity.  Give wisdom to our leaders that they may lead by example. Where we disagree as brethren/acquaintances, teach us reconciliation, lest your name be blasphemed. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 9 — The Queen of Sheba Visits Solomon

### Summary

The Queen of Sheba pays Solomon a visit to prove his wisdom. She brought and got many gifts. Solomon displays his wealth, great ivory throne and chariots and horses. Solomon dies.

### Key Lessons

**1. A city built on a hill**

- The fame of Solomon's wisdom had reached far and wide. The queen of Sheba brought special gifts praised God after being fascinated by Solomon's wisdom.
- Like the Bereans, its always best to convince oneself about any matter.
- Paul operated by the same principle,  saying:_Prove all things; hold fast that which is good._ *(1 Thessalonians 5:21)*
- As Solomon's wisdom attracted many, our Christian walk should, as a city built on a hill not be hid but shine as a light before men.
- The Queen of Sheba considered Solomon's servants blessed (vs 7) for having a wise, kind and considerate king.
- The great need of today is for leaders who reflect the spirit of Heaven, that those under their guidance may find true joy and lasting happiness.

**2. Blessed be the Lord (vs 8)**

- After proving Solomon and finding everything around him breathtaking,  the queen of Sheba said: _"Blessed be the Lord thy God..."_
- How proud would God be if all who come in contact with you left praising Him,  finding,  like in Christ, nothing of the prince of this world.
- For your chastity, your Temperance,  unshakable faith,  your patience, your modesty and your zeal for His mission, God would be blessed.
- When God is shining through you, His blessings will flow towards you.
- Solomon received many presents (vs 13-14, 23-24), silver, gold, raiment, spices, horses
- Solomon was a peace time king, waging no wars but he _" reigned over all kings_" (vs 23, 26). They paid tribute to him.
- True leadership is not in force, but in God given wisdom
- As the Chronicler chose to omit most of Solomon's wrongdoings such as his "grievous yoke" on his people *(2 Chronicles 9:4),* his strange wives, polygamy and idolatry *(1 Kings 11:1-4),* pray that Gods blots out all your sins and cast them to the depth of the sea.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our loving Father in heaven, you promised to give wisdom to them that ask. Grant us thy wisdom. Make us pillars and beacons of faith. May your light so shine through us. Remember not our transgressions and grant us thy peace, in Jesus' name. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 10 — The Kingdom Divides Under Rehoboam

### Summary

Rehoboam,  Solomon's son begins to reign. The counsel of the old against the advise of the young. Jeroboam and Israel revolts against Rehoboam. Kingdom of Israel splits into two.

### Key Lessons

**1. The cause was of God.. (vs 15)**

- After Solomon's death, Israel intended to crown Rehoboam his son King at Shechem.
- Led by Jeroboam, an Ephraimite and former servant of Solomon who had rebelled against the king  having received a prophecy from Ahijah that he would become king *(1 Kings 11:26-40)*
- Having petitioned Rehoboam to lighten their yoke, (tribute and forced labour, *(1 Kings 5:13-14),* Rehoboam threatened to be more severe than his father, disregarding the counsel of the old men.
- Not all advice is good advice. Consulting is good, but wise discretion is critical.
- The words of Job seem to have escaped Rehoboam namely: _"With the ancient is wisdom; and in length of days understanding."_*(Job 12:12)*
- This wisdom though reposes with those who are _found in the way of righteousness._ *(Proverbs 16:31)*
- A soft answer turneth away wrath. Servant leadership is more appealing to the led than dominance.
- Respect is not attained, retained or sustained by force.
- Rehoboam tried to use force in receiving taxes, unfortunately, with tragic consequences. Hadoram was stoned and the king had to flee for his life after the revolt. (vs 18)
- Pride and injustice leads to dissent and disunity.
- God's promises,  good or bad will always come to pass. Israel rebelled against David's house, all because of Solomon's disobedience and Rehoboam's indiscretion.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Loving Father in heaven, we bring before you our leaders today. Teach them to learn of you, to consult you and to do justice. May our elders be found walking in your paths of  righteousness. As they grow old, may they not depart from you. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 11 — Rehoboam Fortifies Judah

### Summary

Rehoboam plans an attack on the seceded northern kingdom but God forbids him. He builds fortified cities. Priests, Levites and adherents to true worship migrate to Jerusalem, forsaking Jeroboam's idolatry.

### Key Lessons

**1. God does not approve (vs 1-12)**

- The ten Northern kingdoms having cut ties with Judah and Benjamin, Rehoboam takes a 180 000 strong army to attack them and forcefully bring them back.
- God through Shemiah said no. Rehoboam relented.
- A potent lesson from Abraham the father of faith saith _"Let there be no strife..., between me and thee...; for we be brethren_
- Brethren should learn resolve their differences amicably and diplomatically and not expose themselves to their common enemies.
- God does not approve of arbitrary actions, an abuse of power or position.
- _Selfish planning and devising is common with those who have never made a full consecration of heart, life, and soul, to God. They move in paths in which Christ does not lead them_ *(Letters 13, Manuscripts 60, 1898, par. 41)*
- Rehoboam went on to fortify his remaining kingdom.
- God should be our primary refuge and strength.

**2. Choose God (vs 12-16)**

- When Jeroboam broke away from Judah, and in order to consolidate his power, he made idols, molten calves.
- He placed one at Bethel and the other at Dan, claiming it was too much for his subjects to go up to Jerusalem and these gods had brought them out of Egypt *(1 Kings 29:26-33).*
- Priests and levites who had been stationed in Levitical cities in the Northern kingdoms migrated south to Jerusalem,  desiring to cling to God's true worship.
- Other adherents to truth from the common people also continued to go up to Jerusalem, fleeing idolatry.
- Regardless of the challenges, choose God.
- Distances, politics, penury or family, job security should be subordinate to God.

## 2 Chronicles 26 — Uzziah's Pride and Downfall

Uzziah begins to reign after Amaziah's death. He does good during prophet Zechariah's time. He is struck with leprosy for offering incense. Jotham succeeds him after his death.

- Be careful to guard against pride.
- Proverbs  16:18  says _Pride goeth before destruction and an haughty spirit before a fall._

## 2 Chronicles 27 — Jotham's Faithful Reign

Jotham's reign, his fortification of Judah, wars and walk with God. He dies and Ahaz succeeds him.

- By prayer, fasting and resisting the devil, we fortify ourselves against Satan's attacks

- A daily walk with God makes one's way prosperous.
- _" If you will leave yourself in His hands, He will bring you off more than conqueror through Him that has loved you."_ *(Ellen G. White: Steps to Christ 71.2)*

## 2 Chronicles 28 — Ahaz's Idolatry and Judah's Affliction

### Summary

Idolatrous Ahaz son of Jotham succeeds his father on the throne. Both Syria and Israel attack Judah, taking captives. Oded the prophet rebukes Israel for taking Jewish bondmen. Tilgathpilneser king of Assyria promises help to Judah but he never came. Ahaz's wickedness continued till death

### Key Lessons

**1. The ways of the kings of Israel**

- Following the good reigns of Amaziah,  Uzziah and Jotham, Ahaz joined the people's wickedness, (27:2) making and serving idols, going as far as burning his children in the fire. This, God had strictly forbidden *(Deuteronomy 18:9-13).*
- Also sad to note is that the kings of Israel, God's chosen, had now become the standard for sin.
- At times, fruits fall far off the tree. The parents’ uprightness may not define a child's path. They make individual choices. Pray earnestly for their chosen life paths.
- Is God's church or its members not a standard of wickedness today?

**2. Wake up calls**

- God had promised to use wars, pestilences, droughts to remind Israel of its sins *(Deuteronomy 28:15-66).* Ahaz headed not the warnings in the Syrian and Israeli wars.
- Heaven sent warnings may be seen in our lives. Let's heed them and prosper.
- Israel under Pekah sought to enslave their Jewish brethren, which was forbidden *(Leviticus 25:39).* Through Oded, God reminded them, no. Thank God for Azariah (vs 12) who successfully implemented God's word to return them.
- Having lived in sin in the past doesn't warrant continued sinfulness. When rebuked, take heed.
- Where necessary,  take it upon yourself to correct other people's wrongs for the good of the rest.
- Time changes,  God doesn't. *(Malachi 3:6, Hebrews 13:8)*

**3. Salvation comes only from the Lord**

- In his distress from the Philistines and others, Ahaz invited Assyria's Tilgathpilneser to help. He promised, but never came.
- Only God is trustworthy. Man doth lie, God doesn't *(Numbers 23:19).*
- Forsaking God means inviting the devil and all he does, like a thief, is to spoil and destroy *(John 10:10).*
- Sin is progressive *(2 Timothy 3:13)* and holiness fades as we move further away from God.
- _"The command is “Depart from iniquity”_ *(2 Timothy 2:19; Ellen G. White: That I May Know Him 255.6)*

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father in heaven, the exceeding sinfulness of sin has paled before us because of our iniquity. Take us back to your paths. Show us where we have fallen and give us the strength to redirect our paths towards salvation. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 29 — Hezekiah Reopens the Temple

Hezekiah opens again the temple doors to the people, sanctifying it and the priests and levites. Many sacrifices are offered. The levites are more forthcoming than the priests.

### Key Lessons

**1. Is your leader guiding you to restoration or ruin?**

- Hezekiah reopens the temple doors which his father Ahaz had shut (28:24, 29:3), sanctifies the priests and levites, reminding them of their appointed roles and how their predecessors had polluted the temple.
- When a leader is evil, the followers are generally bound to be.
- _The whole head is sick, and the whole heart faint_, ....... _from the sole of the foot to the head, there is no soundness_  *(Isaiah 1:5-6)*

## 2 Chronicles 32 — Sennacherib's Siege and Hezekiah's Prayer

### Summary

Sennacherib's army encamps against Judah. Hezekiah,  the princes and the people strengthen themselves in God. Hezekiah and Isaiah pray to God who delivers Judah by an Angel. Sennacherib is assassinated. Hezekiah is healed, sins and repents. After his death, Manasseh reigns.

### Key Lessons

**1. Greater is He that is with us**

- Sennacherib threatened Judah, relying on his recent conquest of Israel.
- Hezekiah tactically starved the Assyrians of water by stopping the fountains that fed the brook, (vs 3-4) encouraged Judah in God's name and together with Isaiah,  prayed to God for deliverance.
- _"...there be more with us than with him, With him is an arm of flesh; but with us is the LORD our God to help us, and to fight our battles.."_
- Surrounded by such a great cloud of witnesses, we can without doubt believe that having God is far better than all armour or powers.

**2. When you leave God's battles in His hands**

- Sennacherib,  by letters and by emissaries, threatened  Hezekiah and all Judah, railing against God as if, like many others, He was' manmade.
- He was also mistaken in thinking that the high places and idols Hezekiah destroyed belonged to God.
- When we compromise the way God ought to be worshiped,  nonbelievers might be misled to believe that's how our God intend that we worship Him.

**3. Guard against pride**

**1. Evil seems to prosper**

- Manasseh, the longest reigning king of Judah had a penchant for evil.

## 2 Chronicles 35 — Josiah's Passover and Death

### Summary

Josiah holds a historic Passover festival. He is unwisely involved in a war with Pharaoh Necho of Egypt and dies of battle woods. A great national mourning is made of him.

### Key Lessons

**1. Celebrating deliverance**

- A great Passover feast is kept at Jerusalem,  followed by the feast of unleavened bread.
- Both king and princes gave liberally and willingly following God's command *(Exodus 23:15, Deuteronomy 16:16)* for all males not to _"come empty handed"_
- The feast emphasised a family oneness where each family or with a neighbor would roast one lamb whole and eat it together, leaving none till the morrow, with loins gird and shoes on feet, a precursor  to the Lord's supper. *(Exodus 12:1-15).*
- The 7 days following Passover would be the feast of unleavened bread, signifying both purity and liberality in giving.
- Passover was a reminder of deliverance from Egyptian bondage. Former gentiles , through Christ,  have been delivered from hopelessness *(Ephesians 12:12)* to His marvelous light *(1 Peter 2:9)*

- When Pharaoh Necho went to war with Assyria at Carchemish, Josiah foolishly and against warning to refrain (Necho actually claiming he was acting on God's orders) disguised himself and joined it. The archers wounded him at Megiddo and he died from the wounds, dying at a young age.

- _Advance not another step in your headstrong, wanton course; for before you are misery and death_ *(Ellen G. White: Testimonies volume 2 562.1)*

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father in heaven, thank you for all the reminders of your goodness and our sinfulness you gave us on our earthly sojourn. Help us to use them to constantly consecrate ourselves anew. May your will help guide our every step in Jesus' name. Amen.


## 2 Chronicles 36 — The Fall of Judah and the Decree of Cyrus

### Summary

This final chapter of the Chronicles records the short reigns of Jehoahaz, exiled to Egypt,  Jehoakim and Jehoakin, exiled to Babylon and finally Zedekiah. Judah continually rejects God. The Jerusalem temple is burnt down. After 70years, Cyrus proclaims the return of captives to Judah.

### Key Lessons

**1. The Limits of God's patience (vs 1-9)**

- All the last 4 kings of Judah were evil, with the first three, Jehoahaz,  Jehoakim and Jehoakin having exceptionally short reigns before being exiled.
- Pharaoh Necho captured Jehoahaz.  Soon after, Nebuchadnezzar in rapid succession,  plundered Jerusalem and exiled Jehoakim and Jehoakin.
- The Lord executes his judgment.
- _The calamities by land and sea, the unsettled state of society, the alarms of war, are portentous. They forecast approaching events of the greatest magnitude. The agencies of evil are combining their forces and consolidating. They are strengthening for the last great crisis. Great changes are soon to take place in our world, and the final movements will be rapid ones._ *(Ellen G. White: Last Day Events 11.2)*

**2. God's compassion spurned (vs 10-16)**

- Jeremiah, _"speaking from the mouth of the Lord"_ was sent to Zedekiah,  entreated him, but he humbled not himself, hardened his heart from turning unto the Lord.
- Out of compassion, for His people and His dwelling place, God sent His messengers, _"rising up betimes"._ They were mocked, His words despised and His prophets misused _"till there was no remedy."_ (vs 16).
- _Ancient Israel suffered calamities on account of their  unsanctified hearts...In their history we have a danger signal lifted before us_. *(Ellen G. White: Last Day Events  60.3)*

**3. Hope for the good (vs 17-23)**

- Nebuchadnezzar came and  mercilessly killed many, burning down God's Temple and carried away its treasures and vessels with the rest of the people.
- Jeremiah's prophecy of the 70 year captivity still stood, *(Jeremiah  25:11-12, 29:10)* providing hope to some, including Daniel *(Daniel 9:1).*
- Cyrus, among other Medo-Persian kings would fulfill the prophecy. (vs 22-23)
- In hope of a soon coming Savior, the earth made new beckons.
- _..In Him we live, and move and have our being.., Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God._ *(Acts 17:28, Hebrews 12:2)*

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, we have sinned against you, forgive us. Thank you for giving us a message of warning and hope. Prepare our hearts to spread it and hasten your coming in Jesus' name. Amen.
