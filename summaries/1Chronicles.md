# First Chronicles

## Introduction

### Title

- The title **“Chronicles”** is an English derivation from the Jerome's Latin Vulgate _"Chronicon"_ literally **"book of events of the days"**
- Originally a single book in Hebrew (the Septuagint) in the same fashion as 1 & 2 Samuel and 1 & 2 Kings, the book was divided into two in the 4th century by the Greek translators.
- The given title **"Paraleipomenon"** meaning "of things omitted" referred to things omitted in Samuel and Kings.

### Authorship

- Similarities in writing style, language and a general point of view suggests unity of authorship with the books of Ezra and Nehemiah.
- Monetary values denominated in Dracs (monetary system introduced by King Darius 1 (522-486BC)), 1 Chronicles 29:7, strongly points to Ezra as the author or that the book was completed during the Persian period.
- David's genealogy also go beyond Zerubbabel (1 Chronicles 3:19-24) who returned to Judea during Cyrus' reign (539-530BC), (Ezra 1:1)

### Historical Setting

- With a period of completion set in the late 5th century BC, the focus of the books of 1st and 2nd Chronicles is not the period they were written.
- Rather, they give an outline record from Adam (creation) to the Persian period.
- Particular focus is placed on the lineage of David in the nation of Judah.

### Theme

- The theme of the books of the Chronicles can aptly be summarized thus, **"that the way of Obedience to the divine standards is the way of peace and prosperity and the way of wickedness is the way of ruin and desolation."**
- The first 9 chapters traces a genealogical outline from Adam to David.
- Details on creation, paradise, the fall, patriarchs, the deluge, Egyptian captivity,  the Exodus, the period of the judges and the reign of Saul are omitted.
- Also omitted are certain events in David's life such as the sin involving Uriahs wife, Adonijah and Absalom's revolts and David's reign at Hebron.
- Focus is placed on the 12 tribes of Israel, then Benjamin and Judah, and finally on the lineage of David, the levites and priests and the city of Jerusalem (Chapter 1-9) David's reign covers the better part of 1st Chronicles (Chapters 11-29)
- Matters of spiritual/religious  interest are given prominence as compared to military and political ones.
- So are the good  kings of Judah whose reigns promoted morality, religious reform and restoration of temple services such as Jehoshaphat, Josiah, Hezekiah among others.

### The Message

- God gave us all the privilege of choice since creation. We ought to choose right and claim our place in the history books of heaven, as victors over sin and not victims of sin.
- Our history begins at the genesis. We ought to heed the message of Jeremiah 6:16, to "stand in the way and see, ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein", lest we get carried away by the tentacles of sin.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father in heaven, thank you for the most logical, most complete, most authentic history of our existence,  your Word. May we grasp that which is to our Salvation from it. In Christ Jesus we pray. Amen.

## 1 Chronicles 2 — The Sons of Israel and the Line of Judah

### Summary

Chapter 2 lists the progenitors of the 12 tribes of Israel,  and promptly shifts focus onto the tribe of Judah, tracing it until David.

### Key Lessons

**1. Preeminence of Judah**

- Even though Judah is not Israel's first born, he is interestingly, deliberately detailed first in the genealogy.
- This is in line with the prophecy given in Genesis 49 as Jacob blesses his children before he died.
- In verse 10, he says concerning Judah, "_The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until Shiloh come; and unto him shall the gathering of the people be.._"
- This "Shiloh" is Christ, who also hailed from the tribe of Judah
- _"The lion, king of the forest, is a fitting symbol of this tribe, from which came David, and the Son of David, Shiloh, the true “Lion of the tribe of Judah,” to whom all powers shall finally bow and all nations render homage."_ *(Ellen G. White: Patriarchs and Prophets 236.3)*

**2. The Onan and Jarha contrast**

- Onan, the son of Judah (v2) tried to avoid fathering children on his brother Er's behalf *(Genesis 38:8-10)* as tradition required. God killed him.
- On the other hand, Jarha, an Egyptian servant stood for his master Sheshan's dignity and raised a family for him through his daughter (v 34-35). He was counted in the genealogy Israel.
- God loathes all forms of wickedness. Both Er and Onan suffered God's wrath.
- We ought to do good even though it doesn't directly benefit us.

**3. The Rechabites, Obedience and Temperance**

- The last verse (55) mentions as scribes an interesting family, the Kenites, _"...that came of Hemath, the father of the house of Rechab."_
- The Kenites, themselves not Israelites, but believed to be of Midianitish origin had historically always shown good naturedness and spiritual *(1 Samuel 15:6, Judges 4:17-21).*
- The Rechabites later were to show a high level of temperance through abstinence from wine, heeding their father's instruction *(Jeremiah 35).*
- If anyone loves God, the Lord can use them regardless of who they are.
- We can also obey our Heavenly Father as the Rechabites obeyed their earthly father. Temperance is key.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father God. Thank you for preserving us, sinful though we have been. May your forbearance lead us to repentance in Jesus' name. Amen

**4. Simeon (v 24)**

## 1 Chronicles 5 — Reuben's Birthright Transferred

### Summary

Chapter 5 details the rejection of Reuben as heir of Israel, transfer of birthright to Joseph and royalty/leadership to Judah. It narrates the victories of the Transjordan tribes namely Reuben, Gad and the half tribe of Manasseh who remained east of the Jordan during the Exodus, through trust in God and their unfortunate exiling by Tiglath-Pileser the Assyrian after their idolatry.

### Key Lessons

**1. Parental blessings/"curses"**

- Reuben, as the firstborn of Israel should have inherited a double portion *(Deuteronomy 21:15-17)* of his father's estate and the birthright. Both were taken from him. (Genesis 39:4)
- A flirting moment of pleasure for a lifetime of disgrace
- We should guard jealously against fleshly lusts. *(1 Peter 2:11)*
- Honouring parents invites blessing. Dishonouring them brings curses *(Genesis 9:25)*
- Joseph through  Ephraim and Manasseh received the birthright *(v 1, Genesis 48:22)* with his sons adopted as Jacob's own *(Genesis  48:22)*
- As Joseph was obedient to Israel his father, Let us be faithful to our Heavenly father.
- Judah shows leadership in that he:
- Saved Joseph's life by suggesting he be sold to Ishmaelites than killed. *(Genesis 37:26)*
- Character and compassion should define us.
- pledged himself before Joseph in Egypt in place of Benjamin to save Jacob from grief *(Genesis 44:18-34)*
- Consider not only your own good,  but also the good of others *(Philippians 2:3-4)*
- Profoundly repented in attesting Tamar is "more righteous than I.." *(Genesis 38:26)*
- Like David, we need to fully admit and repent of our sin *(Psalms  51:3)*

**2. Victory through Christ (1 Corinthians15:57)**

- The three tribes, Gad Reuben and Manasseh,  though valiant and _"skilful in war"_(v 18) were _"helped_" by God to defeat the Hagarites _"for they cried to God in the battle, and He was intreated of them; **for they put their trust in Him**_" (v 22)
- The three tribes continued to expand their territory as promised by God in *(Exodus 23:30)*
- Let us daily purge the little sins that remain in us, until we all come unto perfection,  beholding Christ, spreading His gospel far  and wide.

**3. Apostasy and exiling**

- The Transjordan tribes, ultimately "_went a whoring after the gods of the land_" causing God to allow Pul (Tiglath-Pileser) of Assyria to take them captive, thus they became the first Israelite group to be exiled and scattered.
- Faithfulness to God should be for a lifetime, _unto death_ *(Revelation 2:10b)*

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father God, teach us to abstain from fleshly lusts, to learn to trust in you wholly and may you give us victory in our fight against sin that we may share your holiness. Amen


## 1 Chronicles 6 — The Genealogy of Levi

### Summary

Chapter 6 narrates the genealogy of Levi, divided into the three branches of Gershom, Kohath and Merari his sons. The priestly family of Aaron and his posterity in the Kohathite line and the supporting role in the work of the sanctuary provided by the other Levites, including singers of repute, concluding with the cities of refugee scattered among the other tribes of Israel.

### Key Lessons

**1. Solemn responsibilities**

- Whilst Judah was eminent in the things secular, Levi was eminent on things Spiritual/religious.
- The priestly line of the house of Aaron required special consecration rites/ anointing.
- We have been made a kingdom of priests *(Exodus 19:6),* a royal priesthood *(1 Peter 2:9)* through the unchangeable priesthood of Christ *(Hebrews 7:24)*
- God help you and me to: "_That ye would walk worthy of God, who hath called you unto his kingdom and glory_" *(1 Thessalonians  2:12)*

**2. A God of Order**

- The 3 Levitical branches were given specific roles, (v 48-49) were to encamp around the tabernacle, *(Numbers 1:50, 53)* with specific instructions on how to handle the tabernacle and its instruments *(Numbers 7)*
- Our God is a God of Order, *(Corinthians  14:40)* who from the beginning has demonstrated his love for orderliness.

**3. Intemperance among the elect**

- Within the levitical lineage are a few names which stand out for bad choices and the resultant reward for their actions.
- **Korah** (v 22). Openly rebelled against Moses and Aaron's Godly appointed leadership roles (with Dathan and Abiram) during the exodus.  *(Numbers 16:32)*
- We ought to respect those in authority especially those doing God's bidding.
- **Nadab and Abihu**(v 3), drunk, brought strange fire into the tabernacle and _"a fire from the Lord...devoured them"_ *(Leviticus 10:1-2)*
- Temperance is a must. Whoever is deceived by wine/strong drink is not wise. *(Proverbs 20:1)*
- Evidently, spiritual position does not guarantee personal faithfulness.  We need to "_...work out (your) own salvation with fear and trembling_" *(Philippians 2:12)*

**4. Cities of refuge**

- Within each tribe were designated cities "_that any person that killeth any person unawares may flee thither_" Levites were in charge.
- "_The sinner is exposed to eternal death, until he finds a hiding place in Christ;_" *(Ellen G. White: Patriarchs and Prophets 517.2)*
- We have a duty to point the erring and distraught to Christ, their place of refuge.
- We should not stop evangelising, for each place to have a convenient _"tabernacle_"
- Our places of worship should provide peaceful and welcoming environments for the weary and sin laden...

**5. Ashtaroth (v 71)**

- Ashtoreth, (plural Ashtaroth) a fertility goddess of the Zidonians *(1 Kings 11:15),* had a Canaanite city named after her.
- Evidently this was a centre for her worship.
- Jehovah's name was to be lifted up in a city previously dedicated to a false God through the Levites.
- As new creatures in Christ, He has to be glorified in our bodies, previously given to sin. *(2 Corinthians 5:17; 1 Corinthians 6:20)*

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Lord God, May we value and respect your order in our homes, marriages, society and church. Equip us for your mission and as priests, may we remain faithful to you and duty. Amen.

## 1 Chronicles 11 — David Crowned King Over All Israel

### Summary

Chapter 11. All Israel assembles at Hebron to crown David King. He establishes his throne at Jebus, (later named Jerusalem) after defeating the Jebusites through Joab. A list of his mighty men concludes the chapter.

### Key Lessons

**1. One mind, one purpose. Peace and Progress (v 1-4)**

- David had, after the death of Saul been instructed of God to go and establish himself at Hebron where Judah the tribe anointed him King. *(2 Samuel 2:1-4).* At the Cave of Machpelah where Abraham was buried was there and it was also a Levitical city of refugee.
- After Ishbosheth, Saul's son was assassinated, *(2 Samuel 4:7),* all 12 Israeli tribes sought unification thus anointing David again at Hebron.
- A recognition by "all Israel" of God's anointing on David is to be emulated today.
- In God's church, elected officials deserve both recognition, respect and full support of the laity.
- David under Saul had always behaved normally,  and nobly. All Israel loved him so.
- We need to be able to  continue doing good even though evil is all around us. David led Israel despite Saul's animosity.
- Saul failed in his attempt to kill David because what God has ordained will come to pass. This assurance should give us peace in life.

**2. Incentive to achieve**

- Having set his eyes on Jerusalem (Jebus), David motivated his valiant men of war to capture it by offering a reward, army Chieftancy. Joab emerged victorious. (v 6)
- This was a common biblical practice. David himself had been offered Michal *(1 Samuel 18),* Othniel offered Achsah, Caleb's daughter  *(Joshua 15:16).*
- Pastor Barry Black, USA Senate Chaplain says he and his siblings would get 5c from their mother for each verse memorized, a great influence for his life achievements.
- Our children, (adults too) and church members need to be incentivised to achieve better results.
- Self motivation for God's mission is also key. The devil, like the Jebusites, threatens invincibility,  but with courage and God's guidance,  we are more than conquerors.

**3. Greater and greater with God**

- David had the mind to build a city and did it, _"**...: for the LORD of hosts was with him**_(v 9)
- We need to invite God and we attain much more *(see Luke 1:37; Genesis 26:13).*
- Obedience to God's voice is the key to greatness.

**4. God not a respecter of Persons (Acts 10:34)**

- For us, the new testament ushered in a diversity of spiritual gifts "_For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ:_" *(Ephesians 4:12)* Not for comparisons and competition but for mission advancement.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Lord, we have wandered far and long give us rest. Dwell in us permanently, old and young. May your mission precede our ambition and unity precede uniformity. Amen.


## 1 Chronicles 24 — The Priestly Divisions Established

### Summary

David distributes priestly duties between the Eleazar and Ithamar houses. 24 Divisions established. Levites from Kohath and Merari lines also organised to assist Aaron's line, the priests.

### Key Lessons

**1. Aaron's line, the priests organised**

- David institutes a rotational priesthood, organised around the remaining 2 households of Aaron, Eleazar and Ithamar.
- The arrangement continued even up to the New testament, where we see Zechariah (Luke 1:5),  John the baptist's father being a priest in the Abiah line(vs 10)
- 24 divisions were created, in a 16 to 8 ratio according to the sizes of the families.
- The casting of lots (probably Urim and Thumim) for the whole process shows David found fairness paramount.
- All processes, especially in the house of God, should to a greater degree involve God. Though David planned everything,  the resultant choice was God's through lots.
- Even temple duties require rotation and delegation to allow for rest and eliminate entitlement.
- Shemiah the scribe writing the resolutions before the king, Zadok and Ahimelec shows the level of transparency.
- Record keeping has always mattered to God. "Write in a book" He says *(Exodus 17:14, Isaiah 30:8, Jeremiah 30:2).*
- The rotating mortal priesthood typified the everlasting priesthood of Christ which changes not *(Hebrews 7:22-23)*

**2. Temple support staff**

- Just like in the Tabernacle, all Levites not descendants of Aaron supported the priests in execution of their duties.
- Casting lots, Kohath and Merari's house were also organised as support staff for the priests.
- God loves us all and he has a duty for each and everyone of us and he selects his leaders Himself.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Heavenly Father, help us to be fair, moreso in your house. May we perform our assigned duties to the best of our abilities, whether as leaders or subordinates. Amen.


## 1 Chronicles 25 — The Singers Appointed for Worship

### Summary

24 Divisions of 12 singers selected from Asaph, Herman and Jeduthun's Levitical lines.

### Key Lessons

**1. Music, a form of Prophesying**

- One of David's major strengths was music, with abundant evidence in the Psalms.
- _Music.... rightly employed,..is a precious gift of God, designed to uplift the thoughts to high and noble themes, to inspire and elevate the soul._ *(Ellen G. White: Education 167.4)*
- David separated Asaph, Herman and Jeduthun _"who should prophesy with harps, psalteries and cymbals", "to give thanks and to praise the Lord"_ and _"to lift up the horn", "for the service of the house of God_"
- Evidently,  music could not be separated from the Temple worship,  with 288 specialists (vs 7) and 4000 singers on instruments (23:5)
- "_Singing, as a part of religious service, is as much an act of worship as is prayer. The heart must feel the spirit of the song to give it right expression"_. *(Ellen G. White: Patriarchs and Prophets 594.3)*
- To as far as worship music is Holy Spirit led, it is prophetic.
- Sanctified Levites, like the priests, were chosen to a full time music ministry,  thus speaking to the sacredness of the role.

**2. The Temple, a centre of Unity (vs 1)**

- David deliberately involved Israel's leaders (captains of the host). They needed to feel part of the Temple project.
- Irrespective of the unified state, some might have later felt the temple, geographically having been built within Judah's territory, was not universal.
- During Jeroboam's reign, two molten calves were placed at Bethel and Dan citing "..._It is too much for you to go up to Jerusalem.._" (2 Chronicles 12:26-29)
- The church should be a place where all feel welcome and involved.
- _If there is not unity in the church, Christ is grieved, and the cause of truth is misrepresented to unbelievers_ *(Ellen G. White: Letters 37 par. 2)*

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father in heaven. Teach our ears to cease loving sensuality in music than ennoblement. May I not be a device of division in your church, but a unifier like David. Make my voice lift up thine horn in praise evermore. Amen.


## 1 Chronicles 26 — Gatekeepers, Treasurers, and Officials

### Summary

Potters are allocated divisions, with gate duties assigned by lots. Levites to be in charge of the treasury, officers and judges area selected.

### Key Lessons

**1. Menial but sacred**

- Last group of Levitical temple officials alloted duties include the porters (gatekeepers,) treasurers and judges.
- _Marvellous Grace (vs 1)._ The Korhites were descendants of Korah who in Numbers 16 rebelled against Moses and was swallowed by the ground.
- Remarkably God spared his sons *(Numbers 26:11)* and are notably  counted among porters and Singers *(Psalms 42-49, 84-88).*
- Korah was also progenitor of notables Obed-edom (blessed for his stewardship of the ark), Heman the singer and Samuel the prophet and judge.
- God is able to restore, from rebel family to permanent temple workers and prophets. Fear not your family background. A new page is open.
- Every role in God's house matters. Porters provided security at all entrances to God's house, safeguarding the Ark.
- Avenues to the mind and heart (ears, eyes, tongue) needs be jealously guarded. Proverbs  4:23 says  "_Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life._". Lust, disease and "_strokes"_(Proverbs 18:6) come from these avenues.
- Some in church and families play pivotal gatekeeping roles, defending it from the devil's wiles, standing in the gap though often looked down upon by the more privileged, proud and liberal.

**2. Obscure but deeply involved (vs 24-28)**

- Temple treasury was placed in the hands of Gershom and Eliezer's line.
- All spoils won in battles dedicated by Samuel, David, Saul were placed in their hands.
- Remarkably, till now, descendants of Moses had played obscure roles, unlike Aaron's line, the priests. That they had maintained their integrity Irrespective, to warrant charge of temple treasure is commendable.
- Even in obscure/seemingly minor roles, maintain your integrity

**3. Levites as rulers**

- Hebronite levites, "_among them mighty men of valor_" were placed as rulers of the 3 Transjordan tribes "_for every matter pertaining to God, and all affairs of the king._"
- While Izharites were appointed officers and judges over Israel, the Hebronite Levites were men of valor, in all the business of the Lord and the service of the king.
- They who excel in things God, usually excel in things material.
- Godly men should be in charge of both religious and secular affairs,  as seen in the appointment of the Izharites and the Hebronites. (vs 29, 32)
- God has always desired to be the only ruler/king in mankind's life *(1 Samuel 8:7)*

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Father God, teach us to adore, maintain and respect order. Whatever sacred roles you allot to us, may we be faithful stewards. Everything you bless us with, may we remember to also bless your house. Amen.


## 1 Chronicles 27 — Military and Civil Administration

### Summary

Chapter 27 Chronicles the 12 rotational military captains allocated to each month of the year, the 12 Princes of Israel and other officers responsible for his wealth and some as advisors.

### Key Lessons

**1. Military organization (vs 1-5)**

- 12 Captains were allocated, on a rotational basis, a month each in a year, with each in charge of 24000 men.
- A good 288 000 strong army was ever available for whenever needed.
- Some of the captains, among them Jashobeam, Benaiah, Eleazar and Asahel are among David's mighty men.
- Merit, nothing less, was used to appoint the captains. Salvation and reward require diligence and faithfulness.
- The master in Christ's Luke 19 parable says "_...Occupy till I come.(vs 13)_" and subsequent responsibilities were given in view of performance.
- To us Paul says "_...work out your own salvation with fear and trembling.._*(Philippians 2:12)*
- The inclusion of Asahel suggests the list might date back to David's early days since he was slain by Abner, Saul and Rehoboam's army commander before Israel's unification under David. *(2 Samuel 2:18-23)*

**2. Tribal leaders (vs 16-24)**

- Each of the 12 tribes was headed by an appointed leader.
- The Chronicler omits the 2 tribes of Gad and Asher apparently replacing them with Aaron and Levi, ostensibly,  to maintain the number of the tribes.
- David, in enumerating Israel  left out the under twenties saying "_... because the LORD had said he would increase Israel like to the stars of the heavens._"
- As David evidently leans on the promises of God, taking God at His word,  we too can lean on His everlasting arms. Even when feeling discouraged, rest assured God's silence doesn't mean His absence.
- Godly leadership requires careful administration,  delegation and recognition of all roles.

**3. Overseers of Royal properties (vs 25-31)**

- Stewards were allocated specialised areas including storehouses, vineyards, livestock, orchards etc
- Mankind was given dominion over all creatures, animate or inanimate.
- _We are stewards over everything pertaining to us and Whether we recognize it or not, we are stewards, supplied from God with talents and facilities, and placed in the world to do a work appointed by Him._ *(Ellen G. White: Education 137.5)*

**4. David's Advisors (vs 32-34)**

- Within the king's inner circle were Jonathan (uncle and counselor), Jehiel (tutor of his sons), Ahithophel (advisor), Hushai (friend), and Joab (army commander).
- _Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety._ *(Proverbs 11:4)*
- In the matter of David's census of all Israel *(1 Chronicles 21),* Joab rebuked the king who took no heed and suffered the consequences.
- We do not well if we do not give relevant counsel when it is warranted of us. *(See Proverbs 24:6)*
- Sad to note that Ahithophel once an esteemed counsellor got corrupted by Absalom, providing outrageous counsel to him, only for Hushai to save the day. Ahithophel unfortunately had to commit suicide. *(2 Samuel 17:23)*
- The world indeed is in want of men who would stand for the right, though the heavens fall.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Faithful God, in every sphere of all our lives, may you be the centre. As your stewards, may we be found faithful.  May your promises get fulfillment in our lives. To the discouraged and wounded, strengthen and heal. Deliver us from presumptuous sins. Amen.


## 1 Chronicles 28 — David Charges Solomon to Build the Temple

### Summary

David assembles Israeli leadership, avails Solomon as his successor and God's appointed builder of the temple. Solomon is given the blueprint and urged to do it courageously, listening to God. David hands gold and silver enough for the temple utensils

### Key Lessons

**1. Human desire overruled  by God's will (v 1-4)**

- David assembles Israel's leadership, military and civil and avails Solomon as next King.
- He narrates his original desire to build God a house which was turned down by God for he was a man of war and had shed blood.
- The most notable was the killing of Uriah though most were battle killings.
- Transparency is key for public office. A monarchy was being established by God Himself and all leaders needed to understand its genesis.
- Solomon was not David's eldest son thus the rationale needed explanation.
- Indeed God's thoughts are not our thoughts.
- We make plans, but ultimate success thereof rests in God's hands. All material had been made ready. Only the  builder was yet to come to age.
- In times past, Moses, willing as he was to enter Canaan was ultimately forbidden by God.

**2. Selected for Obedience (v 5-7)**

- Though Solomon was God elected, his success hinged on him being obedient.
- Many Christians today believe in a "_Once saved, always saved_" doctrine, yet God evidently puts a strict condition to everything,  namely,  total Obedience.
- We shouldn't be indifferent to God preferring others over us. Let's strive to be among the chosen, His own special people. Christ is not ashamed to call us brethren *(Hebrews 2:11)* and _God is not ashamed to be called (Our) God, for He hath prepared for (us) a city._ *(Hebrews 11:16)*
- God does not hide it from us, that He wants us to put Him first, to humble ourselves before Him.
- _The only hope for the sinner is to cease from sin. Then his will will be in harmony with the will of Christ...Implicit obedience is the condition of our salvation. God’s will must be obeyed in every particular. It is our salvation to make His law our rule, His life our pattern, His glory our chief end._ *(Ellen G. White: Letters 14 and Manuscripts 73, 1899, par. 6)*

**3. The Charge: _Be strong and do it!_ (vs 8-10)**

- The Chronicler's tone suggests David really pleaded with Solomon to "_keep and seek for all the commandments of the Lord your God..., take heed now..be strong and **do it**._"
- God requires that we serve Him with a perfect heart and willing mind, _for God searcheth all hearts,  and understandeth all the imaginations of the thoughts.."_(vs 9)
- _"Every course of action has a twofold character and importance. It is virtuous or vicious, right or wrong, according to the motive which prompts it._" *(Ellen G. White: Child Guidance 201.3)*

**4. Given by the Spirit, by His Hand (vs 12, 19)**

- In the end, David gives Solomon the pattern/blueprint of the temple, all its instruments, treasures, priestly order.
- In verse 19 "**In writing**"  (Hebrew _Bikhtav_), implies a written record and "**_by his hand_**" (Hebrew _miyyad YHWH_) indicates direct, divine origin or dictation.
- God left nothing to chance. Just like in the original tabernacle, in which Moses had do it "_According to all that i shew thee..._" *(Exodus 25:9, 40).*
- He was even particular with His ten commandments, given to Moses, _"...two tables of testimony,  tables of stone,  **written with the finger of God**_". *(Exodus 31:18)*
- That which pertains to true worship, God jealously wants to be the originator.
- Mankind easily gets carried away. Israel for years continued to worship that brass serpent of Numbers 21, only to be destroyed under Hezekiah's reforms *(2 Kings 18:4).* They could easily have given the glory to David.
- Finally,  David gave Solomon by weight, gold and silver for the furnishings of the temple.
- Interesting to note that while the original tabernacle had one **table** of shewbread, the temple pattern had **tables of shewbread** (vs 16).
- In the end, Solomon made ten of them *(2 Chronicles 4:8)*
- Worshiping God requires a "_precept upon precept_"approach.

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Loving Heavenly Father, Teach us to know our limits. Where you have called us, guide us through, and strengthen us to seek you daily. Equip us with the requisite panoply for the war we are engaged in. Teach us to live not only for ourselves,  but with those coming after us in mind. Amen.


## 1 Chronicles 29 — David's Offering and the Coronation of Solomon

### Summary

David showcases his liberality towards the temple building project, urging others to follow suit, resulting in overwhelming response. David offers praises to God and the congregation join him in burnt and thanks offerings. Solomon once again is crowned King and begins a successful reign. David dies.

### Key Lessons

**1. Leading by example**

- David, in trying to motivate others, not in pride, recounts all the gifts he had willingly given to the temple project.
- Good leaders lead by example.
- _By precept and example,..., he (a minister) can present the truth in a forcible manner._ *(Ellen G. White: Healthful Living 36.3)*
- "The palace is not for man, but for the Lord God." (vs 1)
- Are we careful enough to handle God's affairs more sacredly than we do our own?
- David calls upon all willing to give freely and Israel's chief man and leaders voluntarily gave in abundance,  notwithstanding that David had given so much.
- We should willingly play our part in God's vineyard,  undeterred by the opulence of the privileged. The richness which is from the heart is more precious to God than visible material wealth *(Luke 21:2)*
- God loveth a cheerful giver *(2 Corinthians 9:7)*
- Great joy follows true liberality.  David and Israel rejoiced.
- To God belongs the glory, the power, the greatness, the victory,  the majesty.
- We can never be able to repay Christ for procuring Salvation for us through grace, but we can choose to, "with perfect heart," be willingly generous, giving God His own.

**2. Giving, an act of Worship**

- David, "_in the uprightness of (my) heart..._" gave willingly calling upon God to prepare the congregation's hearts unto Him. Burnt sacrifices followed soon after.
- Giving, freewill or sacrificial, is as much a part of worship as is singing and prayer.
- Psalms  96:8 says  _Give unto the Lord the glory due unto his name: **bring an offering**, and come into his courts._
- All who come before the Lord must consider not taking lightly the act of worshiping through giving.

**3. On the throne of the Lord (vs 23)**

- Solomon is once again crowned King of Israel (the first being 1 Chronicles 23) and is said to "**_seat on the throne of the Lord_**"
- Though the Lord's  throne is Heaven *(Isaiah 66:1),* whoever is called to represent Him on earth sits, as it were, in His throne.
- In Exodus  7:1 _"..the Lord said unto Moses, **See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh:** and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet._"
- We need to realise how sacred the offices we hold in God's house are, seeing we ought to represent God well by our daily contact.
- Solomon's recrowning signified an anticipated enduring unification of all Israel.

**4. A life "full of days"**

- David's life ended on a high note, having fought a good fight to the end, untiringly working towards unity, uplifting Jehovah's Name and true worship.
- Our faith in Christ should endure all hardships and temptations, keeping **_Looking unto Jesus, the author and Finisher of our faith.._** and a crown of life will be given us. *(Hebrews 12:2, Revelation 2:10)*

> 🙏 **Prayer:** For the Spirit of liberality,  we come before you our God. With many and with few, you always accomplish your goals. Cause us to be counted among thine faithful. As with the material, may we also give our hearts unto you daily, that we may attain unto the resurrection of the dead. Amen.
