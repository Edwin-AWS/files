# Esther

## Introduction

### Title
The book is named after its main character, Esther, also known by her Jewish name Hadassah (Esther 2:7).

### Author
The author is unknown.

### Setting
Esther closes the historical section of the Old Testament and is chronologically very close to New Testament times. It is an unusual book, written in Shushan, the capital of Persia (modern Iran) (Esther 1:2). It does not mention God, whereas the secular Persian King Ahasuerus is mentioned over 150 times. There is no direct mention of prayer or worship except fasting (Esther 4:16).

From a human point of view, the Jewish characters appear undeserving of God's mercy, since they did not respond to Jeremiah's prophecy to return to Jerusalem (Jeremiah 29:10-11), Daniel's passionate prayer (Daniel 6:10), or Cyrus's call to rebuild the temple (Ezra 1:4). Yet the book is a practical exposition of God's grace, which protects the innocent and punishes wicked schemes. The setting carries interesting eschatological lessons for these closing phases of earth's history, in which secularism and perverted worship will persecute godly characters.

### Main Themes
- The removal of Queen Vashti from the throne (Chapter 1)
- The appointment of Esther to the Persian throne (Chapter 2)
- Haman's rage (death decree) against Mordecai and the Jews (Chapter 3)
- Grief over the pending genocide, leading to fasting (Chapter 4)
- Esther's crafty feast, which traps Haman (Chapter 5)
- Rewards for integrity and the protection of the Jews (Chapters 6-10)

### Key Takeaways
- Our appearance and position in society are for a divine purpose (4:14).
- It takes spiritual preparation — fasting and prayer — to dismantle satanic schemes (4:16).
- Silence and tact in spiritual warfare are effective, powerful tools (5:6).
- God can prick dead consciences, including those of heathen kings (6:3).
- Integrity and faithfulness like Mordecai's will be rewarded (6:11).
- Wicked schemes, arrogance, and selfishness like Haman's will eventually be punished (7:10).
- Purim is a feast celebrating the deliverance of the Jews from annihilation (9:31).

### Message
*The decree that will finally go forth against the remnant people of God will be very similar to that issued by Ahasuerus against the Jews. Today the enemies of the true church see in the little company keeping the Sabbath commandment a Mordecai at the gate. The reverence of God's people for His law is a constant rebuke to those who have cast off the fear of the Lord and are trampling on His Sabbath.* (Ellen G. White, *Prophets and Kings*, p. 605.2)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, we are deeply sorry that we have broken our promises to faithfully serve You. Yet, Lord, we come into Your presence to seek Your face and the blessings of Your throne. Have mercy on us, and give us Your Spirit so we can better serve You with integrity. Protect us from the evil schemes of the wicked. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 1 — A Vacancy in the Empire

### Summary
Ahasuerus is introduced as a pliable, self-indulgent king, easily manipulated by flawed advisors who help destroy his family.

### Key Lessons

**1. Alcohol Indulgence**
The king spent 180 days (half a year) in a party characterized by debauchery (1:1-9).
- Alcohol is a poison that causes untold personal and business problems. It should be avoided at all costs (Proverbs 23:29-35).

**2. Refusal of the King's Request**
Queen Vashti refused the king's request to be put on public display (1:10-12).
- *Vashti refused to obey the king's command, thinking that when he came to himself he would commend her course of action. But the king had unwise advisers. They argued that it would be a power given to woman that would be to her injury.* (SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 3, p. 1139.5)

**3. Legislative Blundering**
The advisors produced a sham of legislated morality (1:13-22).
- Ahasuerus accepted the legislating of family life because he was a weak leader. The scheme removed Vashti from the throne, and the king lost his best advisor. An elder should be a good leader at home and away (1 Timothy 3:4).

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, we invite the Holy Spirit as we manage our families. Teach us to be temperate at home and in society. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 2 — Esther's Ascension to the Throne

### Summary
Esther ascended to the throne despite being an orphan and a foreigner. The king admired her natural beauty above the artificial enhancements of her competitors. Mordecai exposed Bigthan and Teresh's assassination plot, and an official record was entered in his name.

### Key Lessons

**1. The Vacuum**
Ahasuerus felt the vacuum left by the unwise removal of Vashti from the throne (v. 1).
- It is God's plan that each man should enjoy the intimate companionship of one wife (Genesis 2:18).

**2. The Scheme**
A foolish scheme was used to choose the next queen (vv. 2-4).
- The king's advisors proposed a beauty contest as a means of choosing the queen. Ahasuerus agreed to the plan because he was morally bankrupt.
- Scripture downplays external beauty, calling it vanity, but highlights moral values in the choice of a wife (Proverbs 31:10-31).

**3. Esther's Outstanding Beauty Won the Contest**
Esther joined the queue of virgins vying to be appointed the next queen (vv. 5-20). She won the contest because she used fewer cosmetics and had outstanding natural beauty.
- Cosmetics do not attract principled lovers (Jeremiah 4:30).

**4. Assassination Plot Exposed**
Mordecai exposed an assassination plot against the king (vv. 21-23).
- Mordecai's faithfulness combined with Esther's obedience to protect Ahasuerus.
- Esther displayed outstanding humility from her childhood. God promises to lift up humble, loyal people (James 4:10).

*Those who are humble, and who do their work as unto God, may not make so great a show as do those who are full of bustle and self-importance; but their work counts for more. Often those who make a great parade call attention to self, interposing between the people and God, and their work proves a failure. "Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom: and with all thy getting get understanding. Exalt her, and she shall promote thee: she shall bring thee to honour, when thou dost embrace her."* (Proverbs 4:7-8; Ellen G. White, *The Ministry of Healing*, p. 477.1)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, teach us to be humble and wise in our daily work. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 3 — Haman's Death Decree

### Summary
Haman is quickly promoted by Ahasuerus and becomes a superpower deserving homage. The promotion is undeserved, since it was Mordecai who should have been rewarded for protecting Ahasuerus from the assassination attempt in the previous chapter. Mordecai refuses to pay homage to Haman — not necessarily out of jealousy, but because homage belongs only to God. Haman issues a universal death decree against the Jews, whose distinctive behavior conforms to God's law.

### Key Lessons

**1. Mordecai Refused to Bow Down to Haman (vv. 1-2)**
- We should make personal choices not to disobey God.
- Mordecai's behavior was based on principles embedded in the second commandment (Exodus 20:5).

**2. When the Majority Obey**
The majority of the king's servants, except Mordecai, obeyed the king's orders (v. 3).
- We must be like Mordecai, who was faithful to the king but knew his limits.
- When earthly authorities conflict with God's law, we must obey God rather than men (Acts 5:29).

**3. When God's People Are Hated**
Haman hated the Jews because their laws defied his authority (vv. 4-6).
- This hatred had historical origins, since the Agagites descended from the Amalekites (1 Samuel 15:4-7; Numbers 24:7).

**4. Lots Cast**
Haman cast Pur (lots) before issuing his death decree (v. 7).
- The ungodly are moved by satanic powers in their pursuit of the righteous.

**5. Haman Secretly Crafted the Death Decree (vv. 8-11)**
- Slander and secret meetings are common tools used by wicked people (Psalm 31:13).
- The death decree had the full backing of the king (vv. 12-14).
- In the near future, there will be a similar universal death decree aimed at destroying those who refuse to bow down to an image (Revelation 13:15-17).

*Then I saw the leading men of the earth consulting together, and Satan and his angels busy around them. I saw a writing, copies of which were scattered in different parts of the land, giving orders that unless the saints should yield their peculiar faith, give up the Sabbath, and observe the first day of the week, the people were at liberty, after a certain time, to put them to death.* (Ellen G. White, *Early Writings*, p. 282.2)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, we ask for Your protection in times when the wicked plot the destruction of the righteous. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 4 — Esther's Resolution

### Summary
Mordecai leads a loud cry against the death decree issued in the Persian empire. News reaches Esther in the palace. Mordecai removes any occasion for excuse and compels Esther to intercede for her people. She responds by calling all the Jews to a three-day fast.

### Key Lessons

**1. The Jews Gave the Loud Cry (vv. 1-3)**
- Mordecai led the Jews in a loud cry over the fearful tragedy of the death decree. The storm of persecution will push the remnant church to give the loud cry (Ellen G. White, *Evangelism*, p. 693.2).

**2. Mordecai Refused to Be Consoled by Esther (vv. 4-9)**
- The saints will refuse human consolation during the time of Jacob's trouble (Jeremiah 30:5-7).

**3. Clear Communication**
Esther clearly communicated her constraints in approaching the king (vv. 10-12).
- Esther saw that her mission was impossible without a miracle.
- When faced with difficult assignments, we must exercise faith, knowing that nothing is impossible with God (Luke 1:37).

**4. God's Mission Would Be Accomplished**
Mordecai reminded Esther that God's mission would be accomplished with or without her hand (vv. 13-14).
- It is a privilege to be given duties in ministry. God has placed us in situations to achieve specific, timely tasks (Matthew 24:45-46).

**5. Esther Commits to Fighting for Her People (vv. 15-16)**
- Esther's resolve was supported by communal spiritual revival. Prayer and fasting bring success in spiritual warfare (Matthew 17:21).

*Through Esther the queen the Lord accomplished a mighty deliverance for His people. At a time when it seemed that no power could save them, Esther and the women associated with her, by fasting and prayer and prompt action, met the issue, and brought salvation to their people.* (SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 3, p. 1140.1)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, we pray for a special anointing of the Holy Spirit so we can stand steadfast, unmovable, always abounding in the work of our Lord. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 5 — Esther's Boldness Before the Throne

### Summary
Esther boldly enters the throne room, and the king affectionately holds out the golden scepter. Esther invites the king and Haman to a special banquet. The king senses a hidden agenda, but Esther says nothing about it, instead inviting them to another banquet the next day. Haman enjoys the feast and brags about his privileged status to his family. Haman and his associates design a gallows to hang his enemy, Mordecai.

### Key Lessons

**1. Esther Wins the King's Favor (vv. 1-3)**
- The communal fast in chapter 4 empowered Esther to approach the king fearlessly.
- Spiritual preparation is a necessary prerequisite for boldly approaching God's throne. Like Esther, we will obtain God's favor in our times of need (Hebrews 4:16).

**2. Invitation to the Banquet (vv. 4-8)**
Esther invited Haman and the king to a special banquet.
- Esther wisely selected the place and time to present her petition.
- In our ministry, we must be as wise as serpents and as harmless as doves (Matthew 10:16).

**3. Haman Boasted About His Unholy Achievements (vv. 9-13)**
- Our joy should not be based on wicked works but must be found in Christ (James 5:15-16).

**4. Haman Set Up the Gallows to Execute Mordecai (v. 14)**
- Haman's hatred for Mordecai was fueled by the wicked company he consulted.
- We should avoid corrupting friendships with the wicked (1 Corinthians 5:11).
- We must copy Esther's boldness and not waver in faith.

*He counseled such ones to surrender themselves to God and venture upon His mercy without delay. They would find a gracious Savior ready to present to them the scepter of mercy, even as Ahasuerus offered to Esther the signal of his favor. All that was required of the sinner, trembling in the presence of his Lord, was to put forth the hand of faith and touch the scepter of His grace. That touch insured pardon and peace.* (Ellen G. White, *Life Sketches of James and Ellen White*, 1880, p. 140.2)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, we pray for a special anointing of the Holy Spirit so we can boldly approach Your throne and find grace in our times of need. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 6 — Haman Punished and Mordecai Rewarded

### Summary
Ahasuerus spends a sleepless night reviewing state records and discovers that Mordecai was never rewarded for exposing the assassination plot. Haman walks into the palace expecting to be honored, only to be forced to lavishly honor Mordecai instead.

### Key Lessons

**1. When Sleep Deserts You**
Ahasuerus spent a sleepless night searching the records of the state, and his attention fell on Mordecai's service (vv. 1-3).
- Mordecai's faithfulness was rewarded years after the service was rendered. Our faithfulness will be rewarded when the heavenly records are reviewed by God (Revelation 20:12).

**2. Just in Time**
Haman walked into the court just in time to receive instructions from the king (vv. 4-5).
- God coordinates our movements, and everything is beautiful in its time (Ecclesiastes 3:11).

**3. When Pride Kicks In**
Haman's selfishness led him to pronounce the perfect reward for faithful service (vv. 6-9).
- We must avoid pride, because it often comes before a terrible fall (Proverbs 16:18).

**4. Rewards Are Assured**
Mordecai's reward was delivered by his worst enemy (vv. 10-14).
- God reverses our enemies' plans when our ways please Him (Proverbs 16:7).

*What a scene will be presented when the judgment shall sit and the books shall be opened to testify the salvation or the loss of all souls! It will require the unerring decision of One who has lived in humanity, loved humanity, given His life for humanity, to make the final appropriation of the rewards to the loyal righteous, and the punishment of the disobedient, the disloyal, and the unrighteous. The Son of God is entrusted with the complete measurement of every individual's action and responsibility. To those who have been partakers of other men's sins and have acted against God's decision, it will be a most awfully solemn scene.* (Ellen G. White, *Faith and Works*, p. 17.4)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, teach us to be humble and to wait for Your punishment of the wicked and rewards for the righteous. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 7 — Haman Hanged on His Own Gallows

### Summary
Esther reveals her Jewish identity at the second banquet and intercedes for her people, who were about to be executed by Haman's decree. Haman is hanged on the gallows he had built to execute Mordecai.

### Key Lessons

**1. When Kings Encourage Disclosure**
Haman and Ahasuerus attended Esther's second banquet (vv. 1-2).
- The king again pressed Esther to disclose her request. The king's attitude encourages us to ask for help (John 14:13).

**2. Esther Interceded for Her People (vv. 3-6)**
- Esther boldly identified Haman as the enemy.
- The devil is our adversary, and we must boldly resist his work (1 Peter 5:8-9).

**3. When the Plea Comes Too Late**
Haman's plea for his life was honored neither by Esther nor by the king (vv. 7-8).
- We must avoid God's wrath and irrevocable punishment (Revelation 14:10).

**4. Punishment for Wickedness**
Haman was hanged on the gallows he had built to execute Mordecai (vv. 9-10).
- The wicked normally fall into the traps they set for the righteous (Psalm 9:15).
- Haman was inspired by Satan and did not repent.

*When God's messages of warning are brought home to the conscience, Satan leads men to justify themselves and to seek the sympathy of others in their course of sin. Instead of correcting their errors, they excite indignation against the reprover, as if he were the sole cause of the difficulty. From the days of righteous Abel to our own time, such is the spirit which has been displayed toward those who dare to condemn sin.* (Ellen G. White, *The Great Controversy*, p. 500.1)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, teach us to be humble and to wait for Your punishment of the wicked and rewards for the righteous. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 8 — Esther Secures an Irrevocable Seal

### Summary
Esther secures Mordecai's promotion to the position created by Haman's death. She then places the king's seal of authority in the hands of the Jews, who reverse the death decree against them. There is universal joy as justice is enthroned.

### Key Lessons

**1. Shared Power**
Esther receives power from the king and shares it with the Jews (vv. 1-2).
- Christ, like Esther, shares His power and dominion over Satan with His church (Ephesians 1:20-22).

**2. Esther Reverses Haman's Death Decree (vv. 3-7)**
- Haman's decree had to be reversed because it pronounced death upon God's people.
- The church that is connected with Christ, like Esther, saves sinners from death (Mark 16:16).

**3. The King Issued an Irreversible Decree (vv. 8-14)**
- God is our Lawgiver (Isaiah 33:22), and He has given His disciples an irrevocable law (Isaiah 8:16), in which we find His Sabbath — an irrevocable seal (Ezekiel 20:20).

**4. When the Godly Reign**
There was peace, love, and joy when Mordecai began to reign (vv. 15-17).
- There will be peace, love, and joy when Christ begins to reign (Isaiah 9:6).

*All who keep the seventh day signify by this act that they are worshipers of Jehovah. Thus the Sabbath is the sign of man's allegiance to God as long as there are any upon the earth to serve Him. The fourth commandment is the only one of all the ten in which are found both the name and the title of the Lawgiver. It is the only one that shows by whose authority the law is given. Thus it contains the seal of God, affixed to His law as evidence of its authenticity and binding force.* (Ellen G. White, *Patriarchs and Prophets*, p. 307.2)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, we ask that You quickly put an end to the reign of lawlessness. Seal us with Your irrevocable sign. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 9 — Tears Turned into Joy

### Summary
Every Jew had been terrified by Haman's approaching Pur, but God mysteriously turned their fear into joy. A new feast, Purim, displaced Pur. Haman's ten sons and other enemies were executed on the very date and in the very manner they had planned to use against the Jews.

### Key Lessons

**1. When the King Is on Your Side**
The king assisted the Jews against their enemies (9:1-5).
- The King of kings is on our side; we should not fear the enemy's threats and maneuvers (Hebrews 13:6).

**2. Fight the Enemy, Not Plunder Them**
The Jews destroyed their enemies but did not loot their property (vv. 6-10).
- The Jews' integrity was rooted in the example of Abraham, who did not plunder his enemies' goods (Genesis 14:22-24).
- We should not be driven by covetousness even in spiritual warfare (Hebrews 13:5).

**3. Esther's Influence**
Esther influenced the king to completely eradicate Haman's household (vv. 11-16).
- Esther's example teaches us to seek complete victory over the enemies of truth.
- Joshua began the battle against the Amalekites in the desert (Exodus 17:8-16); Saul secured only a partial victory over them (1 Samuel 15:18-19); Esther completely destroyed them.

**4. Celebrate Victories**
The Jews assembled to celebrate their victory (vv. 17-19).
- The Jewish community joined together to celebrate God's victory over the enemy.
- We look with anticipation to the day when God's people will assemble to celebrate victory over sin (Revelation 19:6-9).

**5. Mordecai and Esther Institute Purim (vv. 20-32)**
- Purim was instituted as the day God gave the Jews rest from the pursuit of their enemies. God will soon deliver His people in a similar way.

*It is at midnight that God manifests His power for the deliverance of His people. The sun appears, shining in its strength. Signs and wonders follow in quick succession. The wicked look with terror and amazement upon the scene, while the righteous behold with solemn joy the tokens of their deliverance. Everything in nature seems turned out of its course. The streams cease to flow. Dark, heavy clouds come up and clash against each other. In the midst of the angry heavens is one clear space of indescribable glory, whence comes the voice of God like the sound of many waters, saying: "It is done." Revelation 16:17.* (Ellen G. White, *The Great Controversy*, p. 636.2)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, keep us faithful, loyal, and distinct as we wait for the Second Coming of Christ. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

## Esther 10 — Mordecai's Promotion

### Summary
This short closing chapter shows the greatness of the Medo-Persian empire and of Mordecai, who reigns alongside the king, to the joy and prosperity of his people.

### Key Lessons

**1. Mordecai's Promotion**
Mordecai was promoted to a high position in a great empire (vv. 1-2).
- Mordecai's promotion is but a small reflection of Jesus's ascension to heaven, where He is now enthroned (Acts 2:32-33).

**2. When the God-Fearing Are Promoted**
Mordecai's promotion benefited the Jewish community (v. 3).
- Christ's ascension benefits the Christian community (John 16:7).
- At His coronation, Christ gave us a complete package of gifts to edify the church (Ephesians 4:11-12).
- The ascension of Jesus was met with loud, joyful music, which must be echoed by His brethren on earth.

*From that scene of heavenly joy there comes back to us on earth the echo of Christ's own wonderful words: "I ascend unto My Father, and your Father; and to My God, and your God." John 20:17. The family of heaven and the family of earth are one. For us our Lord ascended, and for us He lives. "Wherefore He is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by Him, seeing He ever liveth to make intercession for them." Hebrews 7:25.* (Ellen G. White, *The Desire of Ages*, p. 835.1)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, give us the joy of serving with Christ. May the Holy Spirit anoint us daily while we eagerly await the appearance of our Master. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.

### Conclusion

**Closing Esther**
Esther closes the twelve historical books of the Old Testament. It is set in the highly secular Persian empire. The Jews — a remnant — are threatened with extermination but mysteriously overcome the storm. God turned Haman's witchcraft (Pur) into Mordecai's popular deliverance feast (Purim). The miraculous deliverance of the Jews reassures every believer that a despised remnant shall win the great controversy between good and evil, between God and Satan.

**Nuggets from Esther**
*1. God can use anyone in the Great Controversy*
- He used Esther — a woman, an orphan, and a foreigner (2:7).
- He used Mordecai — a Benjamite and a fugitive (2:5).

*2. God positions us to win*
- He allowed Vashti to vacate the palace.
- He made Esther win the beauty contest.
- He gave the Jews eleven months to prepare for their planned extermination.
- He gave wisdom and courage to Esther and Mordecai.
- He orchestrated the execution of the wicked Haman.
- He gave the Jews access to the highest office in Persia.

The book is a precious gem for the closing stages of earth's history, characterized by despotic leaders of secular societies. As in the days of Esther, a remnant that keeps the commandments of God (Revelation 12:17) will be threatened but will ultimately be saved.

*The decree that will finally go forth against the remnant people of God will be very similar to that issued by Ahasuerus against the Jews. Today the enemies of the true church see in the little company keeping the Sabbath commandment a Mordecai at the gate. The reverence of God's people for His law is a constant rebuke to those who have cast off the fear of the Lord and are trampling on His Sabbath.* (Ellen G. White, *Prophets and Kings*, p. 605.2)

> 🙏 **Prayer:** Our Father in heaven, we thank You for taking us through our study of Esther. We ask for Your Holy Spirit so we can have wisdom and courage to face the storm ahead of us. Protect us from the evil schemes of the wicked. We pray in the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
